A lane's uncommitted work and its unmerged commits now survive a clean quit. Before, `release` and a dropped lane cleared the slot's session even when the tree was dirty. The next claim then took the slot as free and ran `checkout -f -B` and `clean -qfd` over the only copy of the work. A resumed session that landed on a free or fresh slot had its own unmerged branch reset to `main` by `-B`. D130's rule, that a dirty tree is not free, held only for the orphan a crash leaves.

Now a quit or a drop over a dirty tree leaves the slot as its session's orphan: the branch stays checked out, `yi lanes` lists it, a full pool counts it, and that session's resume takes it back ahead of any free slot. The dirty read names untracked files even under a `status.showUntrackedFiles=no` config. A session-less slot whose tree is dirty, the shape an older yi left behind, is counted and listed as an orphan too, never wiped. A claim never runs `-B` over a branch the base does not contain; it checks that branch out instead, on a reused slot and on a fresh one. A quit that keeps a dirty slot prints which slot holds the work and the reap that would discard it. A reap and `/discard` are the only ways a tree is cleaned, and each says how many uncommitted paths it dropped ("lane 0 freed; 2 uncommitted paths discarded"), as a floor with the cap named when the status list passed it. `yi doctor --fix` reaps only an orphan with no uncommitted paths and reports the others with the reap that would discard them. Settling a worktree candidate and keeping a staging pin go through the discard path, so whatever their commit left out is cleaned when the slot is handed back rather than at the next claim.
