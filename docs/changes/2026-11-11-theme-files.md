Yi's palette can now be a theme. `theme.dark` and `theme.light` in `~/.yi/config.json` name a theme for that ground: one of the 104 that ship in the binary (the 100 oh-my-pi themes, whose MIT notice is in NOTICE, and four new ones, chalk, ember, iris and tide) or `~/.yi/themes/<name>.json` in oh-my-pi's shape, which wins over a shipped theme of the same name. The theme replaces the colors Yi already paints: accent, text, muted, dim, error, warning and success, the user-message and selection grounds, the syntax roles, the added and removed diff lines (drawn on Yi's own tints, and back to the built-in colour wherever the theme's falls under 3:1 against the tint), and the markdown, status and thinking colors. Pane borders, title chips and scroll thumbs keep their session hue. A 256-color terminal gets the nearest cube color for each, a 16-color terminal keeps its own colors and says so once.

In the console, Dark theme and Light theme in the settings pane open the list under the row; moving through it paints the console and every open chat, Enter saves, and Esc, another row, a moved focus or a closed pane put the palette back. The row for the other ground saves without painting. In the solo TUI, `/theme` does the same for this terminal's ground. A preview repaints the live rows only, so scrollback and a pane's scroll position stay where they were, and a save rewrites the transcript in the new colors the way a resize does.

A theme file that cannot load keeps the built-in palette and names why: a name that is not a var, a chain through more than 16 vars, a file past 256 KB, or a path that is not a file. The shipped light-prism named a `muted` var it never defined, upstream too; its copy here defines it.
