A history hit shows its line in the case it was written. Before, `compact.recall` lowered the whole snippet and cut it at 160 characters without saying so, and `compact.search` cut at the same width with no word of it either. Now a hit cut to the 160-character window carries `"cut": true`, and the reply's `notice` gains a row that names the cap and the call that reads the rest: `[1 of 1 snippets cut to 160 chars (SNIPPET_CHARS) · rlm.fetch(hit["url"]) reads a cut hit whole]`. A `compact.recall` hit gains a `url`, `history://self/<entry>`, since it had no address to read; a `compact.search` hit keeps `history://<session>/<entry>`. The window is centred on the match by walking the original text, so a letter that lowercases to two characters no longer pushes the match out of view.

A reader whose wall refuses a cut hit's fetch, at its `history://self` address or under this session's id or name, is not sent to a fetch its wall refuses: the same row says the wall puts the rest of a cut hit out of reach. A wall on another transcript only, such as `history://main`, leaves the fetch row in place.

Both verbs now build their reply in one `paged_reply`, which spells the page row, the snippet row and the unsearched-directories row the same way. A page of exactly 32 hits has no page row and a 33rd names `offset=32`, above the snippet row when its hits are cut. `history.grep` and its reply moved from `wiring.rs` into `history.rs` as `register_grep`, which leaves `wiring.rs` 106 lines shorter. The `Past sessions` paragraph in `docs/memory.md` and one feature-ledger row carry the new reply.
