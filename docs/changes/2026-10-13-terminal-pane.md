⌘Y (⌥y) opens a terminal pane beside the focused pane (Closes #1378). It runs `$SHELL`, falling back to `/bin/zsh` then `/bin/sh`, in the focused session's working directory, which is its lane worktree once `_yi/workdir` has named one, else the session's root, else the console's. The console owns the PTY and parses it with `vt100`; the child is `yi __pty <shell>`, which calls `setsid` and `TIOCSCTTY` and then execs the shell, with no unsafe code. The pane draws the shell's cells with their colours and attributes and shows its cursor when focused, the mouse wheel scrolls its scrollback, and a resize reaches the shell as `tcsetwinsize`. A focused terminal takes every key except ⌘ chords, ⌥ arrows (the focus move) and the ctrl+b table, and a paste is bracketed when the shell asked for it. Closing the pane sends SIGHUP to the shell's process group, and a shell that exits leaves the pane titled `[exited N]` until it is closed. The Tape pane leaves under §1.2's one-in-one-out rule, with its key, its `_yi/tape` route and the worker's fold. Input never blocks the console loop: keys and pastes go over a 64-entry queue to one writer thread, and when a child stops reading fills the PTY and then the queue, the next keystroke fails with a visible "input queue full" note instead of buffering without bound. The residual risk that remains: that writer thread sits in `write_all` on the full PTY holding its master clone until the pane's drop kills the shell's process group and the write fails.
