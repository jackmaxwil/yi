Reclaim decides a cut in tokens on every route. The price test (`tokens dropped × cache-read price × requests` against `tokens after the first cut × (write − read price)`) left a route whose catalog prices cache at $0 (a subscription such as the Kimi lanes) unable to ever cut, since its read price was zero. A cut now pays when the tokens it drops, times the requests so far, exceed ten reads of the tokens after the first cut result (`REWRITE_READS`, a constant, not a setting), and a batch must drop at least 20,000 tokens (`MIN_DROPPED`, was 2,000). A lapsed cache still cuts at once, at the old 2,000-token floor. Dollar rates no longer enter the rule; the stored session is untouched. The simulator run over the newest lane sessions and the Kimi lane A/B with `yi catalog usage kimi` before and after the cut need the network and the lane's credentials, and are not part of this change: they are the gate the issue names and are still owed.
