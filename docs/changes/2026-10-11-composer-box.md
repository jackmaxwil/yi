The composer is a box now, not a line. A paragraph typed without a newline word-wraps at the box's width instead of scrolling sideways, and the box grows with it in steps of 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 and 21 text rows, each about the golden ratio past the last, so a long paste moves the layout a handful of times rather than once per row. It never takes more than 38.2% of its pane, so the transcript keeps the golden share: 20 text rows on a fullscreen 16-inch MacBook in Ghostty (about 215 by 60 cells), 10 in Ghostty's default window, 7 in an 80 by 24 Terminal.app or iTerm2 window. An empty box rests at three rows on a pane of 24 rows or more and one row below that. Up and Down walk the wrapped rows at the same column. Solo and the console's chat pane paint the same composer, so both change together.

The editing keys follow the shell and macOS now, in the composer and in the console's notebook and file editor alike. Ghostty sends Cmd+Delete as ctrl+u, which the textarea read as undo: ctrl+u and Cmd+Delete (super+backspace under the kitty protocol) clear the cursor's line, and undo moves to ctrl+z with ctrl+shift+z to redo. A terminal without kitty flags reports Shift+Enter as Enter, and its ctrl+j deleted to the line's head: ctrl+j and Option+Enter insert a newline. Option+Left and Option+Right move by word over a draft and walk the agent tree only from an empty box; the console's alt+b, which Ghostty sends for Option+Left, gives way to word-left over a draft rather than hiding the sidebar. Cmd+arrows under the kitty protocol go to the line's ends and the text's top and bottom, ctrl+backspace and ctrl+delete delete a word, and the textarea's emacs paging on ctrl+v and alt+v is gone.
