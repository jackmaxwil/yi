The plan tool takes the shapes models actually send (Closes #982). Twelve dogfood sessions on glm-5.3-flash made 146 plan calls and had 32 refused for shape alone. A call with no `op` but a `goal` and `todos` is an init, one with a `list` a set; `set` takes a `todos` array as its checklist and, with no plan open, opens one named after its first row; `init` without a `goal` is named after its first todo; a deadline written beside `cmd` moves inside it; `fail` takes `reason` as its cause; `done` or `decompose` on the owner's own pending todo starts it first. A second round of the same sessions found the next layer, and it lands too: a missing weight is 1 and weights past 100 are rescaled with their ratios kept; a `done` whose `output` is prose keeps it as the note; `decompose` written as a flag or a nested object, and `todos` sent as a JSON string, are read as meant; a checklist's headings, blank and struck rows are skipped and a set prunes `after` edges to rows it removed; `append` with no plan open opens one; a partial `reorder` is completed from the plan's own order; annotations such as `retry`'s `reason` are dropped with a note naming them. A third round found the last layer: `labels` lists rows for an op that takes rows and runs one call per todo for an op that takes one; the op's own key holding the todo or the arguments is read beside `op`; a contract item with an empty decider is left out, and a contract left empty with it; `supersede` without a reason records `none given`, and with no plan open opens one; `unblock` on a todo that is not blocked changes nothing; `fail` or `retry` on the owner's own pending todo starts it; `todos` sent as a string that is not JSON says where the parse failed; a git failure no longer prints its exit as `Some(128)`. A fourth round added: a checklist line that is not a row leaves the list and a box `set` cannot read is unchecked; a `plan` naming no open plan runs the call on the open one; prose `output` holding a `://` is a note; `supersede` or `append` on a finished plan opens a new one. A fifth round found new keys at the same rate, so the owner chose a rule over more rounds (amends D194): a key the op does not read is left out and named in a note, while a key with a hint, one a single typo from a known key (`contarct`, `outpt`) and `actor` still refuse; `delegation` written once beside `todos` goes to each todo, a `label` on `append` is its one row, `block`'s `question` becomes its note, a prose `disposition` is left out, and a `plan` the store has no file for runs on the open plan. A sixth round left no unknown-key refusal and added: `todos` with no op is an append, a contract without a class is `inline`, `block` without a note takes the todo's label, and a finished sub-plan's supersede is refused as not active instead of trying to open a plan beside its open parent. A seventh round left one recurring shape, a todo declared with `on`: it is now created and then blocked by its own call. Strict tool schemas at the provider are #995. `drop`'s `reason` and `done`'s `note` are accepted and the reply says they stay in the call's arguments. Refusals that remain say where a field goes (a todo's `label` inside `todos`, blocking as its own op, a todo's children through decompose), name the todo at its index, list only the ops a model may call, quote labels as written instead of `TodoLabel(\"…\")`, and no longer point a tool caller at the kernel's `Plan.attach` alone; the no-confirmer reply says to report the check instead of asking again.
