A child's card sits under the kernel cell that was running when the host admitted it, by record and not by timing. The spawn line a parent journals on its own transcript (`ChildSpawned`) gains an optional `spawnedBy`, the tool call id of the cell that ran `rlm.run`; the field is additive, so an older line decodes with it absent. `KernelBridge::execute_cell` takes the call's id, the kernel service holds it while the cell runs (the loop runs an exec call alone, so one cell runs at a time), and the subagent host reads it when it admits a child, writing it to the trail line and to the roster's `ChildView`. The TUI adopts a child under the cell its record names; before, it took whichever `ipython` cell was running when the roster first listed the child, so a child the first cell spawned, first seen while the second ran, waited on the second and committed under it. The record names the cell running at admission, not the cell that asked: a spawn from outside any cell (the plan timer's dispatch backstop runs off the loop's turns) records the model's cell if one is running then, and none otherwise. A child with no cell recorded (a spawn outside any cell, the console's forwarded updates, a host older than the field) still uses the timing guess; that is no worse than before. A child's completion mail stays quiet after its card is dropped by a reset or the cap, as it did while every card lived forever. A committed card releases its cached answer rows, since only a live card draws from them. `crates/runtime/src/kernel.rs` grows by two lines (1,188 to 1,190) for the slot.

Child cards now end with the transcript they belong to and stop piling up. A `/new` or a rewind cleared the history and the live region but kept every card, so `/agents`, the alt-arrow focus cycle and the status count went on listing the children of an earlier transcript, and each card's feed held its child's whole session alive. `reset_transcript` moves from `app.rs` to `app/children.rs` and now also drops the cards, the order, the committed set and the focus; the dropped ids are remembered, so the next roster tick brings back a child that is still running and leaves a finished one in the old transcript. Finished cards past 32 leave oldest first once they are committed to scrollback, never a running card and never the focused one, and `/agents` then ends with `[…] agents: 32 of 33 finished shown · cap 32 finished children · older cards are in the transcript`. `commit_finished_tasks`, which runs on every child update, cloned the whole order and every card (up to 16 KB of answer each) before it looked at a status; it now checks each card by reference and clones only the one it commits. `app.rs` goes from 1,098 lines to 1,095.

A running child card renders its answer once per change, not once per frame. Each frame cloned every running card (up to 16 KB of answer) and ran markdown over the whole answer to keep its last four rows, and every child delta asked for a frame, so K streaming children cost K full renders a frame. `TaskState` now keeps the card's answer rows (`AnswerRows`), reused while the answer text, the width and the theme are unchanged, and `TaskCell::lines_at` takes the running time as an argument instead of a cloned card; the title, the step rows and the fade are still drawn each frame. The rows are reused only for the same text, not the same length: a card at its 16 KiB cap keeps its length while the text moves. Measured with eight running cards holding 16 KiB answers at width 100 over 200 frames: 31.2 ms a frame in debug and 12.4 ms in dist before, 0.50 ms in debug after. The rows are checked against the uncached renderer's output recorded at the head before the change (`crates/tui/tests/fixtures/task-card-rows.json`).

Two members of this bundle were killed by their zero-spend tests and are not built. E-uilife-history would have dropped the `details` of tool cells older than the resize replay cap. Its test replayed the five recorded sessions under `crates/tui/tests/fixtures/sessions` (40 tool cells), summed each cell's retained strings and values (its arguments, result text and `details`), and projected 10,000 cells at the 95th-percentile cell, 16,981 B: 161.9 MiB, under the 256 MiB bar, so a session of that length holds less than a browser tab and the cut is not worth its risk. E-uiperf-delta would have appended a streamed delta to the live strings instead of rebuilding them from the whole message. Its test fed a 64 KiB answer as 3,276 deltas of 20 B in a debug build: the rebuild inside `arrive` cost 2.4 ms in all, under the 50 ms bar, while the whole delta path cost about 90 ms, nearly all of it the linear commit scan the unit does not touch; a build with the append measured no gain at 64 KiB.
