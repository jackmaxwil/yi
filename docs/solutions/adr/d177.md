# D177: `--deadline` is enforced, not only shown

Status: accepted

## Decision

`--deadline` is enforced, not only shown: the session holds one `Deadline`, its
start and total in a `OnceLock` that `attach_runtime` sets once, and the environment
line, the tool cancel and the loop's stop all read it; at zero the tool cancel fires
as Esc does, so bash's watchdog kills the process group (0.214.0) and the kernel
watcher interrupts the cell (D176); `should_stop_after_turn` ends the run after the
turn in flight once 240 s, or a quarter of a shorter budget, is all that is left,
never cutting a turn or a stream, so the turn settles and `yi ask` exits 0; a child
session starts its own clock from the same total, and a headless TUI drive's
`--deadline` now bounds its session as well as its script; `yi ask` reads on past a
lagged event stream, the count on stderr, where it exited 0 mid-turn (amends D150)

## Why

D150 put the clock in the environment block and nothing read it, so a command or a
kernel cell that outlived the budget held its turn until harbor killed the container
(the 2026-09-10 harness audit, B6); the margin is 240 s, not 120 s, because the
longest request measured, a settled reasoning cut, ran about 170 s, and a request
that starts inside the margin must still finish before harbor's kill; a flat 240 s
margin is longer than the 120 s and 180 s budgets of `answer-echo`, `edit-file`,
`clean-workspace` and the three live fixtures, and would end each of their runs
after its first turn, so a short budget keeps three quarters of itself

## Reversible via

Drop `session/deadline.rs` and the `OnceLock`: the cancel flag reads the interrupt
alone, `should_stop_after_turn` stays unset, the environment line keeps its own
`Instant`, and the `yi ask` loop breaks on any receive error
