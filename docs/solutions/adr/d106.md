# D106: the forge is the only register of planned work

Status: accepted

## Decision

Planned work lives on `git.example.invalid/apex/yi` as issues, grouped by
milestones and arranged on a project board. An issue number is the identity of
a piece of work: `#N`, not a row id in a file, not a heading in a plan.
`docs/TODOS.md` stops being authored — it is header-marked frozen and moved to
`docs/archive/todos-2026-09-02.md`, where its only remaining job is to be the
provenance of the issue bodies that were seeded from it.

The mechanics that follow from it:

- A feature pull request names its issue in the body — `Closes #N` when the
  merge finishes the work, `Refs #N` when it is one part of it — and the
  changelog row for that version cites the same `#N`. The merge closes the
  issue; nobody closes one by hand.
- Sizes are labels. Exactly one of `size:S` (≤ 1 day), `size:M` (≤ 3 days) or
  `size:L` (larger) is mandatory, beside at least one `area:*` label and a
  milestone.
- Milestone due dates are arithmetic: a weekly job divides the milestone's open
  size-days by measured throughput and writes the date. A person never types
  one.
- The board's columns — Backlog, Next, In progress, Blocked, Done — are queries
  over the issues they hold, and Done is reached by merging, not by dragging.
- Enforcement lives in CI. No tracking check runs offline, and a green
  `just check` says nothing about whether the work is registered.

## Why

The people are on the forge and a file cannot hold a contributor's issue. A row
in `docs/TODOS.md` cannot be assigned, cannot be commented on, cannot be opened
by anyone without a commit bit, and cannot be edited by two sessions at once
without a collision — this tree has eleven worktrees against one `.git`, and the
register was a merge conflict waiting for a second author.

The closing loop is native. `Closes #N` on a merged pull request closes the
issue with no second actor and no second edit. The file's equivalent was a
`**DONE <version>**` marker typed by hand, which nothing checked and which a
landing could simply forget; `docs/forge-planning.md` measured 52 of 127 rows
marked done and could only map 47 of them to a date.

And dates become measurable. An issue carries its own open and close timestamps,
so throughput is read off the register directly. The file's version could not:
`docs/forge-planning.md` had to join `DONE` markers to changelog dates by hand,
lost every row's size the moment it was marked done, and had to state outright
that its 7.83 rows/day divisor was the one number in the document that was
assumed rather than measured. That assumption is what a milestone's due date was
being computed from.

Enforcement is CI's because only CI can reach the forge. A gate that asked the
forge a question offline would either block a plane-ride build or answer by
guessing, and a guessing gate makes `just check` lie about something it never
checked.

## Reversible via

A register regenerated from the forge. Each issue carries its seed row's text
verbatim and its `done:` gate, so `tea issues ls --state all` plus the issue
bodies renders the file again — the archived copy is the format it renders into.
