# D109: panic and dead-artifact gates judge behaviour, not spelling

Status: accepted

## Decision

A gate that matches the spelling an author chose only ever catches the authors
who spell it that way. Two classes move to gates that judge behaviour:

- Byte slicing of a `str` is a lint, not a review habit. Every crate root
  carries `#![deny(clippy::string_slice)]` unless the crate is named in
  `scripts/guardrails/baselines/string_slice_pending.json`, a list that only
  shrinks; `check_manifests.py` fails a crate whose root has neither. There is
  no `allow` and no `expect` for the lint: a crate is clean and denied, or it
  is pending with an open issue. `String::truncate` is a disallowed method in
  `clippy.toml` for the same reason — take `chars().take(n)`, `strip_suffix`,
  or walk back on `is_char_boundary`.
- A `pub` field is written to be read, and a baseline exists to be opened.
  `scripts/guardrails/check_orphans.py` holds both at zero: a `pub` field
  declared in a `struct` under `crates/*/src` that no line in the workspace
  reads, and a file under `baselines/` whose name appears in no file under
  scripts/, evals/, skills/ or the justfile.
  `crates/types` is exempt (its fields are read by the wire, not by us), as is
  any struct deriving `Serialize` or `Deserialize`. A test counts as a reader.
  Its `--selfcheck` disables each scan in turn and asserts the selfcheck fails
  for that scan's own reason.

`grid orphans` stays a review step rather than a gate: its unit is the
definition, and a field is not one.

## Why

`check_panic.py` is a regex over `.unwrap()|.expect(|panic!|todo!|unimplemented!`.
It saw none of `error.truncate(80)` on a wire-sourced child error, which panics
the render thread the first time byte 80 lands inside a multi-byte character.
The behaviour — a byte offset used as a character offset — is exactly what
`clippy::string_slice` and a disallowed `String::truncate` see, and neither
depends on how the call was written.

Nothing in the tree looked for artifacts that had stopped being read. rustc's
`dead_code` sees nothing `pub`, so a `pub` field crosses the crate boundary and
is never read again with no warning anywhere: `SourceBudgets.advisories` and
`emergency_ceiling` were carried and defaulted but never enforced, a status
threshold was computed for nobody, and `ApplyResult.block_resolutions` was
filled with an empty `Vec` at two call sites and read at none.
`baselines/file_size_budget.json` was a 0-byte file that no script ever opened:
added at 0.97.0 on 2026-08-31 and still unread 26 versions and 178 commits
later, because `check_file_size.py` hardcodes its 1,200-line limit.

## Reversible via

Remove the crate-root attributes and the `clippy.toml` entry; delete
`check_orphans.py`, its `run` line in `check_guardrails.sh`, and
`baselines/string_slice_pending.json`.
